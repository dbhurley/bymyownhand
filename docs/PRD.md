# By My Own Hand — Product Requirements Document

> **Last updated:** 2026-08-11
> **Status:** Living document — evolves with the roadmap
> **Owner:** DB Hurley

---

## 1. Vision

In an era where any sentence can be machine-generated in milliseconds, **the act of writing — keystroke by keystroke — is one of the few remaining proofs of human presence.** By My Own Hand exists so writers, students, journalists, professionals, and creators can prove their words came from their own hands and own minds.

> *"Your words deserve proof."*

---

## 2. Problem

| Audience | The pain |
|---|---|
| **Students & academics** | Schools cannot reliably distinguish AI-written submissions from human-written ones. False positives from AI-detection tools punish honest writers. |
| **Journalists & writers** | "Was this written by a human?" is now an editorial question. There is no portable, verifiable proof of authorship. |
| **Professionals & knowledge workers** | Cover letters, statements of purpose, internal memos, and creative briefs all need to demonstrate human authorship — but no standard exists. |
| **Creators & bloggers** | AI content floods the open web. Human writers want a visible signal that their work is hand-composed. |
| **Platforms** | Publishers, hiring teams, and educators want a low-friction way to verify human authorship without invasive surveillance. |

---

## 3. Target Audience (priority order)

1. **Writers & creators** publishing on personal sites, Substack, Medium — want a "verified human" badge alongside their post.
2. **Students** submitting essays who want a defensible record of their writing process.
3. **Educators** asking students to compose drafts in a verifiable environment.
4. **Job seekers** submitting written application materials (cover letters, essays, take-home assignments).
5. **Journalists & op-ed writers** whose work depends on the credibility of human voice.

---

## 4. Core Value Proposition

A **lockdown editor** that captures every keystroke with millisecond timing, blocks all external paste, and produces a **shareable, tamper-evident verification link** plus a **downloadable certificate** — proving a piece of writing was composed by a real human in real time.

The output is not a probability score from an AI detector; it is **a recording of the act of writing**, replayable on demand.

---

## 5. Product Pillars

| Pillar | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Provable** | Every claim ties back to a keystroke trace. Anyone with the link can replay the writing as it happened. |
| **Frictionless** | Open the page, write, click submit, get a link. No accounts required for the MVP path. |
| **Portable** | The proof lives at a stable URL, can be embedded as a badge, and downloaded as a PDF certificate. |
| **Private by default** | We capture timing, not biometrics. Documents are never used for AI training. |
| **Aesthetic** | Writers are an aesthetic-sensitive audience. The editor must feel like a calm, premium writing surface — not surveillance software. |

## 6. Current Capabilities (shipped)

### 6.1 Locked writing editor
- Monaco-based plaintext editor with calm typography (Bricolage Grotesque, large line-height).
- **Paste blocking:** external clipboard content is intercepted on *every* route into the editor — Cmd+V/Ctrl+V, mobile long-press → Paste, `Shift+Insert`, middle-click paste, and the browser's Edit ▸ Paste menu — and each attempt is counted and written to the keystroke trace. The guards run from `window` in the capture phase so they are reached before the editor's own clipboard handling, whatever the route (see §6.68 — registering them on the editor's DOM node was one level too low, and the non-keyboard routes were reaching the document unguarded). Only intra-editor copy/paste is permitted, on every route: the keyboard path allows a paste when the editor holds the writer's own copied text, and the non-keyboard paths allow it when the system clipboard's content is exactly that text (preserves accessibility for moving text within a draft).
- **Drag-and-drop blocking:** Files and external text dropped onto the editor are rejected and counted.
- **Quiet recording indicator:** A pulsing dot signals the session is active.
- **Live word count, elapsed timer, blocked-paste counter** in the toolbar.
- **Submission gate:** ≥10 words required before "Complete" is enabled.

### 6.2 Keystroke capture
- Each event recorded with `{ t (ms-from-session-start), type, key, pos, len? }`.
- Event types: `key`, `delete`, `paste_blocked`, `paste_internal`.
- **Every removal is captured, not just Backspace/Delete.** A cut — via `Cmd/Ctrl+X`, the browser's Edit ▸ Cut menu, or a mobile long-press → Cut — is recorded as a `delete` carrying `len`, the number of characters it took out.
- **Undo and redo are captured too.** Hooked on the editor's model-change event (scoped by `isUndoing`/`isRedoing`), so an undo records the `delete` it performs and a redo the bulk re-insertion, each carrying `len` and a `key` of `Undo`/`Redo`.
- **A replaced selection is captured as the removal it is.** Typing over selected text, backspacing over it, or pasting the writer's own copied text over it removes the whole selection — the trace records that removal with its `len` (`key: 'Replace'` for a typed-over or pasted-over selection, `Backspace`/`Delete` where that key caused it) alongside the insertion that followed. Every known mutation of the buffer is now in the trace, which is the prerequisite Phase 3.4's content-reconstruction check was waiting on.

### 6.3 Metrics + integrity score
Computed at submission time:
- Average keystroke interval, keystroke variance (CoV), pause count (>2s gaps), deletion rate, blocked-paste count, longest uninterrupted burst.
- A 0–100 **integrity score** that penalizes blocked pastes, super-human typing speed, robotic variance, and missing pauses/deletions.

### 6.4 Verification & sharing
- `bmoh-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx` verification hash (nanoid, URL-safe).
- Shareable verification page at `/verify/<hash>` with:
  - Word count, duration, integrity score, blocked-paste count.
  - **Keystroke playback:** the document is replayed character-by-character at 3× speed.
  - Detailed metrics panel.
- Success page at `/success/<hash>` with copy-link and "Write another."
- Downloadable PDF certificate (`@react-pdf/renderer`), rendered in PDF's built-in standard fonts so it generates offline and makes no third-party request at download time (see §6.69 — registering a remote web font broke the download outright for any title containing an em dash).
- Optional Neon Postgres persistence for the MVP demo flow; when `DATABASE_URL` is configured, certification succeeds only after the durable write succeeds, while an intentionally unconfigured demo can still use the same-session browser handoff.

### 6.5 Content & SEO
- 44+ long-form blog posts on identity, AI, and content provenance.
- JSON Feed at `/api/blog/feed.json`.
- `llms.txt`, `humans.txt`, full Open Graph + Twitter card metadata, canonical URL via `metadataBase`.

### 6.8 Reliability + config polish (prior revision)

- **Honor `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` everywhere** — certificate verify links, the embedded QR code, and the on-page share copy now resolve through the documented env var instead of a hard-coded production domain. Staging, custom domains, and locally-served previews all produce correct proof links.

### 6.9 Stickiness + fidelity polish (prior revision)
- **Cold-visitor share + embed on `/verify/<hash>`** — the share/embed surface that previously only lived on `/success` is now mirrored on the public verification page, so anyone arriving via a shared link can copy the proof link, post to X/LinkedIn, or grab the Markdown badge to embed. Closes the second-order distribution gap that was blocking the embed flywheel.
- **Faithful-cased keystroke playback** — the `/verify/<hash>` playback used to reconstruct the document from raw key codes (`KeyH` → `h`), so a piece titled "Hello" played back as "hello" before snapping to the final cased text. Playback is now driven positionally from the certified content while keystroke timings still control the typing/deletion rhythm. Result: the playback matches the document the visitor is verifying, end-to-end.
- **Single `getSiteUrl()` helper** — the four-times-duplicated `process.env.NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL || 'https://bymyownhand.com'` pattern is collapsed into one helper in `lib/site.ts`. The `/success` embed badge image URL was the last place still hard-coded to the production domain (so a staging-generated badge pointed its logo at production); it now honors the env var like everything else.

### 6.10 Discovery + embed-format polish (prior revision)
- **Real `/sitemap.xml` and `/robots.txt` routes** — `public/robots.txt` had been pointing at a `/sitemap.xml` URL that 404'd, so search engines couldn't discover the 44+ blog posts or the marketing surfaces. Both are now generated from `app/sitemap.ts` and `app/robots.ts`, resolved through `getSiteUrl()`, and the sitemap enumerates `/`, `/write`, `/blog`, and every blog post with appropriate `lastModified` and `changeFrequency` hints. Discovery surface for the cross-cutting "Documentation" investment in the roadmap.
- **HTML embed snippet alongside Markdown** — Phase 1.3 (Embeddable badge) now ships a Markdown ↔ HTML toggle on both `/success` and `/verify/<hash>`. WordPress, raw-HTML CMSes, and email signatures accept HTML but strip Markdown; a single-toggle UX widens the set of platforms where the embed flywheel can take root before the full `embed.js` and `iframe` variants ship.
- **Surface `averageWordLength` in writing-analysis panels** — the metric was already computed (§6.7) but never displayed; it's now shown on both `/success/<hash>` and `/verify/<hash>` so the panel reflects all the data we actually capture.
- **Drop the fabricated `integrityScore = 75` fallback on `/verify/<hash>`** — for a document with no keystroke trace (legacy, or trace stripped) the verify page used to render a confident-looking "75 / Good" score. It now renders an explicit "— / No trace" cell so a verifier can never mistake silence for evidence.

### 6.11 Performance + API + embed-validity polish (prior revision)
- **Memoize `getAllPosts()` in `lib/blog.ts`** — `getAllPosts()` is the single source for sitemap, JSON Feed, blog index, individual posts, related-posts, categories, and tags. Each call re-read all 44+ markdown files from disk and re-ran `marked` over them; a single render of `/blog/<slug>` was triggering this work several times. A module-scope cache makes the first call canonical and every subsequent call free, materially reducing cold-start work for the blog index, the post page, and the sitemap that ships every certified URL to search engines.
- **Absolute `verifyUrl` from `POST /api/documents`** — the create-document endpoint returned a relative `/verify/<hash>` URL, which the in-app web client doesn't use (it constructs its own from `window.location.origin`). External API consumers (Phase 2.1) need a full URL they can share unmodified. The route now returns `${getSiteUrl()}/verify/<hash>` so the shape matches the documented Phase 2.1 contract before the public API ships.
- **Valid `height` on the HTML embed snippet** — the HTML badge snippet on `/success` and `/verify/<hash>` rendered `<img ... height="auto" />`. HTML's `height` attribute requires a non-negative integer; `"auto"` is invalid and gets stripped by stricter CMS sanitizers (and tarnishes any HTML-validator scan a publisher might run on the post). Replaced with a real numeric height (`107`, computed from the `363×324` logo at `width=120`) so the badge renders cleanly across every embed target the toggle is meant to support.
- **Sitemap `/blog` `lastModified` anchored to the latest post date** — the blog index entry was emitting `lastModified: new Date()` on every crawl, which is a freshness lie: the page never changes between posts. Search engines re-spent crawl budget on a static index instead of on the new posts they'd actually discover. The entry now uses the most-recent post's date so the index's freshness signal tracks real content changes.

### 6.12 Habit-loop + drift-prevention polish (prior revision)
- **Local-first writing streak on `/success/<hash>`** — first surface for Phase 1.4 (Writing streaks) before the optional accounts in Phase 1.2 land. A new `lib/history.ts` records each certification (hash, ts, word count, score) into `localStorage`; the success page reads back the total count and the consecutive-day streak and renders a calm pill — *"3 certified pieces · 2-day streak"* — directly under the "Document Certified" header. Recording is idempotent on hash, so revisits don't double-count. Same staged-rollout pattern as Phase 1.3 (Markdown badge first, full `embed.js` later): we start the habit-formation feedback loop on day one and migrate to a server-synced shape when accounts ship.
- **Single `buildEmbedSnippets(verifyUrl)` helper in `lib/embed.ts`** — the markdown + HTML embed-badge snippets were defined inline in *both* `success/[hash]` and `verify/[hash]` with the same `width=120, height=107` aspect-ratio comment in each copy. A future tweak (alt-text wording, query-string tracking, a v2 logo URL) had two places to drift from. The snippet generator now lives in one helper, mirroring the prior `getScoreLabel` / `getSiteUrl` consolidations. Drift-prevention before the script-tag and iframe variants land in Phase 1.3.
- **Defensive blog-date handling in `sitemap.ts` and `feed.json`** — posts with a missing or unparseable `date` used to fall through into the sitemap with `lastModified=now` (the same freshness-lie pattern §6.11 just fixed for `/blog`) and into the JSON Feed with a literal `"T00:00:00Z"` `date_published` (which most feed validators reject — a bad signal for Phase 4.1's opt-in feed work and any partner ingesting the feed). Both routes now skip such posts entirely rather than emit a misleading entry. Today's content corpus all parses cleanly; the change protects discovery surface against a future post landing without a date in frontmatter.

### 6.76 Certification-boundary honesty + retry-safety polish (this revision)
- **Configured persistence now fails closed, with the draft intact** — `POST /api/documents` used to catch a failed database INSERT and still return a verification hash. The writer saw “Document Certified,” but the proof existed only in their `sessionStorage`; every cold visitor received a 404. An intentionally unconfigured MVP still uses the browser-only demo path, but once `DATABASE_URL` is present a failed write returns the same retryable `503` + `no-store` contract as the read route. The editor receives a real failure and keeps autosaving the draft for retry.
- **Malformed JSON is a client error, not a service failure** — an empty, truncated, non-object, or syntactically invalid request body previously fell into the route-wide exception handler and returned 500. Parsing and top-level-shape checks now return actionable 400 responses before the certification logic runs, sharpening the contract Phase 2.1 partners will eventually consume.
- **The editor validates the certification response before clearing work** — a 2xx response with a missing/malformed `verificationHash` or `documentId` previously routed to `/success/undefined`, then allowed the editor to delete its only local draft. The response is now treated as untrusted: both identifiers must pass their real runtime contracts before the session handoff, navigation, or draft-finalized signal can happen.
- **Non-JSON infrastructure errors keep a useful retry message** — a proxy/CDN 502 or 503 can be HTML or empty. The editor no longer lets `response.json()` throw a parsing detail that masks the actual status; it falls back to a calm status-based certification error while the retained draft continues autosaving. `isValidVerificationHash()` now accepts `unknown` as a real type predicate so this response boundary does not need an unsafe pre-cast.

### 6.75 Browser-persistence trust-boundary polish (prior revision)
- **One complete success-handoff contract on both destination pages** — `/success/<hash>` previously accepted any parsed object with the right hash, while `/verify/<hash>` checked only the hash, content type, and start time before handing the rest to metrics and playback. A shared `SessionHandoff` validator now requires the complete app-minted shape: IDs/hash/title/content, coherent timing, canonical word count, bounded score, valid metrics, and a fully valid keystroke trace. The writer cannot land on a half-rendered success page or send malformed local events into playback because the two routes no longer disagree about what the same payload means.
- **Blocked browser storage now follows the durable fallback path** — both readers called `sessionStorage.getItem()` outside their parse guards, so a privacy mode that denied storage access could throw before the redirect/API fallback ran. Shared `readSessionHandoff()` and `writeSessionHandoff()` helpers catch access, parse, quota, and validation failures alike. Certification still counts locally when the write handoff cannot be stored, and both destination pages degrade to `/verify` rather than strand the writer.
- **Resumable drafts validate the events they promise to preserve** — the draft schema checked only that `events` was an array, despite resuming it directly into metrics/certification. It now reuses the API's canonical `isValidKeystrokeEvent()` predicate for every event and also rejects empty session IDs, non-positive timestamps, and negative/fractional blocked-paste counts. A corrupt local draft is pruned before it can crash or poison the resumed proof.
- **Clock corrections cannot turn timing variance negative** — event timestamps intentionally tolerate a device clock moving backwards, but `calculateMetrics()` divided the positive standard deviation by a signed mean interval. A sufficiently large correction produced a negative coefficient of variation, falsely triggering the robotic-rhythm penalty and failing the new handoff contract. The denominator now uses the mean interval's magnitude, keeping the metric non-negative without discarding the timing evidence.
- **Proof-history titles reuse the canonical document-title limit** — `history.ts` still carried its own literal `200` one revision after the editor/API title contract was centralized. It now imports `MAX_DOCUMENT_TITLE_LENGTH`, so future title-policy changes cannot make recalled proof names drift from the proof itself.

### 6.74 Title identity + durable-history + success-handoff polish (prior revision)
- **One canonical 200-character title contract from editor to proof** — the API capped titles at 200 characters, but the editor did not: `/success`, share copy, and local proof history kept the raw client title while the durable DB record, certificate, and `/verify` page silently used the truncated server title. A single `MAX_DOCUMENT_TITLE_LENGTH` constant now drives the API and the editor's `maxLength`; the write handoff trims/caps before both POST and sessionStorage, and restored legacy drafts are capped too. One proof now keeps one title across every surface.
- **Proof-history totals now describe what the device actually keeps** — local history retains the newest 500 certifications, but entry 501 was summarized before the cap was applied. The success pill briefly said 501 pieces and its recall copy calculated from 501 even though only 500 links were recoverable; a reload then changed the total back. `recordCertification()` now persists and summarizes the same capped collection, keeping the habit signal honest at its storage boundary.
- **The success handoff no longer violates React's effect contract** — reading `sessionStorage` and immediately cascading four state updates from the effect body tripped `react-hooks/set-state-in-effect`, leaving the repository's lint command red. The external-storage reconciliation now runs from a cancellable post-effect callback, preserving the same warm-session render and cold-session redirect while removing the synchronous cascade. `pnpm lint` returns zero errors (the 12 existing `no-img-element` advisories remain warnings).

### 6.73 The badge fragment shipped the whole application + a 49 KB mark for 22 pixels + a database hiccup told a reader the proof did not exist (prior revision)
- **The `iframe` badge fragment was shipping the entire app shell to every reader (`app/embed/[hash]/route.ts`, replacing `page.tsx`)** — `/embed/<hash>` was an ordinary Next.js page, so it inherited the root layout, and the root layout *is* the application. Measured against the production build, the fragment carried the React runtime and seven more client chunks, the site's Tailwind stylesheet, **two `rel="preload"` `next/font` woff2 files**, the web-app manifest, six icon links, the JSON-Feed autodiscovery link, and a canonical pointing at the homepage: **14,458 bytes of HTML pulling ~671 KB of subresources**, to render a 197×36 pill of text and one 22px image. The fonts are the sharpest instance — `BADGE_PILL_CSS` sets `system-ui`, so Bricolage Grotesque and Geist Mono (64 KB of woff2, preloaded, therefore fetched whether or not anything renders in them) were downloaded and never used — and the route's own comment promised "no client JS, no fonts to fetch," a claim the built output had quietly made false. This is the badge format fetched by every *reader* of every page carrying it, on someone else's page and over their connection, so the cost scaled with precisely the adoption §9's "live verification badges in the wild" metric measures. It is now a **route handler emitting its own complete document**: a route handler has no layout to inherit, so what a reader receives is exactly what the file writes, and no change elsewhere in the app can add to it later — the property this surface actually needs. Verified against the production build: **751 bytes**, 0 scripts, 1 inline stylesheet, one network resource; framed on a dark third-party host page the pill still measures 197×36 over a transparent body with no padding, and a malformed hash still renders genuinely nothing (527 bytes, empty `<body>`). ISR is preserved (`● /embed/[hash]`, `x-nextjs-cache: HIT` on the second request) alongside an explicit `Cache-Control` identical to `/embed.js`, and `noindex` moved from a `metadata` export to an `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, follow` header. `pnpm build` passes.
- **The badge mark was a 49 KB, 192×192 PNG painted at 22 pixels (`lib/embed.ts`, `app/embed.js/route.ts`, `app/embed/[hash]/route.ts`)** — both served variants pointed at `/icon-192x192.png`, the app icon, which the pill draws at 22 CSS pixels: a ~50× oversample. Since the pill's own markup is a few hundred bytes, the mark *was* the badge's weight — the single file that made a "small trust badge" cost 49 KB on every third-party page it landed on. `/badge-mark-44x44.png` is a straight downsample of the same icon at 2× the rendered size (so still crisp on retina) and weighs **2,696 bytes**: visually identical at 22px, 94.5% smaller. Deliberately a second constant beside `BADGE_MARK_PATH` rather than a replacement — the copied Markdown and HTML snippets render the mark at 120px, where downsampling the source would be a visible regression, so those keep the 192px icon. Verified on a third-party host page: both served variants load the new mark, the script pill still measures 196.7×36, `alt=""` / `target="_blank"` / `rel="noopener"` / the absolute `/verify/<hash>` href are unchanged, and the `/success` Markdown snippet still emits the 192px icon. With the fix above, a reader meeting an iframe badge for the first time transfers ~3.4 KB where they used to transfer ~685 KB.
- **A database failure told every reader that a real proof did not exist (`app/api/documents/[hash]/route.ts`, `app/verify/[hash]/page.tsx`)** — the route caught a Neon error, logged it, and fell through to the `404 Document not found` at the bottom of the handler. `/verify/<hash>` renders that as *"This verification link is invalid or the document has been removed."* So a dropped connection, an exhausted connection pool, or a few seconds of maintenance did not merely degrade the proof page — it **repudiated the document**, on the one surface whose entire purpose is to make a claim about that document, in front of the cold visitors the embed flywheel exists to send there. A badge in the wild cannot know it briefly linked to a page denying its own piece, and the writer, who shared the link days ago, never finds out; a reader has no way to tell an outage from a fabricated certification. The route now answers `503 { error, retryable: true }` with `Cache-Control: no-store` — the success response is deliberately cached for a day, and caching this one would outlive the outage that produced it — and `/verify` reads the *status* rather than the presence of an error string: `404` means not found, anything else means the service could not answer, as does a failed `fetch` (offline, DNS, a dropped connection), which says nothing about whether the document exists either. The unavailable state gets its own calm heading, an explicit *"This is not a judgement on the document — the link is unchanged"*, a neutral dash in place of the cross, and a **Try again** button that re-runs the load rather than demanding a page reload. Verified end-to-end against the production build with `DATABASE_URL` pointed at an unreachable host: `503` + `no-store` from the API, "Verification Temporarily Unavailable" on the page, *Try again* issuing a second request; and on the ordinary no-DB build a genuine miss still renders "Document Not Found" with no retry button, the `sessionStorage` fast path still renders a full proof page after a real certification, and `/verify/garbage` still emits `noindex`. 0 console errors. `pnpm build` passes; lint unchanged from baseline (13 problems — the one pre-existing `/success` setState-in-effect error and 12 pre-existing `<img>` warnings).

### 6.72 A share card that fetched the whole keystroke trace + the one served badge nobody cached + an autosave that rewrote an unchanged trace every three seconds (prior revision)
- **The proof page's share card pulled the entire document — keystroke trace included — to read one title (`lib/db.ts`, `app/verify/[hash]/layout.tsx`)** — §6.59 gave `/verify/<hash>` a per-document OG/Twitter card by resolving the document title in `generateMetadata`, and it resolved it through `getDocumentByHash()`, which is a `SELECT *`. The widest column that returns is `keystroke_data` — the JSONB trace, bounded only by the 250k-event cap the API enforces — followed by the full `content`. The block reads one ~40-character string off that row and discards everything else. The route is server-rendered on demand, so the whole trace crossed the wire from Neon on **every** request to a proof page: every social-scraper unfurl of a shared link, every crawler fetch, and every human pageload. That is exactly the traffic Phase 1.3's embed flywheel exists to manufacture, which makes this the wrong shape of cost in two directions at once — the better the flywheel works the more it charges, and the charge scales with how much the writer actually wrote, so the longest and most impressive pieces were the most expensive ones to show off. A new `getDocumentTitleByHash()` projects the single column, behind the same `isValidVerificationHash()` gate as the wide read so no external roundtrip is spent on input that can't have been minted by us. `getDocumentByHash()` is deliberately untouched: `GET /api/documents/[hash]` needs the trace, and it is already edge-cached (§6.13). Verified against the production build: `/verify/<valid-hash>` still emits its self-referential canonical and falls back to the generic card on the no-DB path, `/verify/garbage` still emits `noindex`, no console errors. The DB-backed title branch is logically unchanged but was **not** exercised end-to-end — this environment has no `DATABASE_URL` and no local Postgres. `pnpm build` passes.
- **The `iframe` badge was the one served variant nobody cached (`app/embed/[hash]/page.tsx`)** — what `/embed/<hash>` renders is decided entirely by the *format* of the hash in its URL: a well-formed one gets the labelled pill, anything else gets nothing (§6.71). There is no per-request state, no database read, and no reason its output for a given URL should ever differ between two requests. It was nonetheless `ƒ (Dynamic) — server-rendered on demand` in every build, while `/embed.js` — which renders the same badge from the same constants in `lib/embed.ts` — is static and cached at the edge for a day. Two variants of one artifact, one free and one billed per impression. And the asymmetry fell on the worse of the two: `/embed.js` is fetched once per *page*, whereas the frame is fetched by every *reader* of every page carrying the badge, so the format that succeeded most was the format that cost most — a flywheel whose running cost grows linearly with its own success, on the metric ("live verification badges in the wild") §9 names as the flywheel itself. The route now declares a 24h `revalidate` and an empty `generateStaticParams` — the deliberate "no prebuilt paths, cache on first request" shape, since there is no build-time list of hashes to enumerate and `dynamicParams` admits every hash writers actually mint. The revalidate window matches what `/embed.js` already advertises, so a change to the shared badge definition still reaches embeds in the wild on one schedule rather than two. Verified against the production build: the route moved from `ƒ` to `●` in the build output; a valid hash serves `x-nextjs-cache: HIT` with `Cache-Control: s-maxage=86400, stale-while-revalidate=31449600`; a malformed hash serves `HIT` from the second request onward; and framed on a dark third-party host page both render exactly as before — the 197×36 labelled pill for the valid hash, genuinely nothing for the invalid one, no cream slab. `/embed.js` is byte-unchanged.

### 6.22 Resume-timeline + draft-schema + dead-hash + sitemap-freshness polish (prior revision)

The value now comes from the same `countWords()` helper that gates the threshold, so the persisted count and the contract that admitted the document agree by construction. Trust-boundary fix: the server is now the canonical source for both the gate and the record.
- **Absolute `verifyUrl` SSR fallback on `/success/<hash>`** — the success page falls back to a relative `/verify/<hash>` URL when `typeof window === 'undefined'` (initial server render), while `/verify/<hash>` was already using `getSiteUrl()` for the same fallback. The mismatch means the embed-badge snippet emitted in initial HTML on `/success` carries a relative proof link until hydration replaces it. Now both pages resolve the SSR fallback through `getSiteUrl()`. Drift-prevention sibling of the prior `getSiteUrl` / `buildEmbedSnippets` / `getScoreLabel` / `countWords` consolidations.
- **`blog.ts` uses the shared `countWords()` helper** — `parsePost()` was computing the post's read-time from a bespoke `body.split(/\s+/).length` (counts an empty leading whitespace token as a word; doesn't trim). The shared `countWords()` helper exists exactly to keep word-counting consistent across surfaces and was already in use in the editor, the resume banner, the API gate, and the metrics path. Blog read-time now goes through the same contract — the same kind of consolidation as §6.13's removal of the five duplicated regexes.

### 6.14 Verify-honesty + SEO-emission + cold-success polish (prior revision)
- **No fabricated "0 Pastes Blocked" on `/verify/<hash>` for trace-less docs** — §6.10 already replaced the fabricated `integrityScore = 75` fallback with an explicit "— / No trace" cell. The `Pastes Blocked` cell on the same row was still rendering a confident `0` for the same trace-less documents (`{metrics?.blockedPastes || 0}`), painting "we ran the paste-blocking check and saw zero attempts" when in fact we have no evidence either way. The cell now mirrors the integrity-score treatment: render `metrics.blockedPastes` when a trace exists, otherwise show "— / No trace". Closes the second half of the same honesty principle in one place.
- **JSON-LD on `/write` no longer hidden behind a submission-error pill** — the four `<script type="application/ld+json">` blocks recently injected on `/write` were nested *inside* the `{error && (...)}` conditional that wraps the red error pill. The page only emitted structured data when a submission attempt had just failed — i.e., never on a normal crawl. The scripts also referenced a nonexistent `/images/logo.png` URL. They now render unconditionally as siblings of the header (with the duplicate Article block dropped because the homepage already publishes one, and the broken logo URL replaced with the real `/logo.svg`). Search engines and AI scrapers see the structured data on every load instead of only the error path.
- **Cold `/success/<hash>` redirects to `/verify/<hash>`** — opening `/success/<hash>` from a bookmark or after `sessionStorage` had been cleared used to render a placeholder card showing the user's own document with `0 words / 0s / 0 Low` stats — confusing, and worse, suggesting a fresh certification had just been recorded (which `recordCertification()` would then have written to the local streak history with bogus zeros). The page now `router.replace()`s to `/verify/<hash>` whenever `lastSession` is missing or its hash doesn't match the URL — `/verify/<hash>` already knows how to fetch from the DB or fail clearly when neither source has the document. Same shape as the other "no fabricated evidence" fixes.
- **Variance formatting parity between `/success` and `/verify`** — `/verify` rendered keystroke variance as `metrics.keystrokeVariance.toFixed(2)` (always two decimals, e.g. `0.20`); `/success` rendered the same metric as `String(session.metrics.keystrokeVariance)` (raw, e.g. `0.2`). Visually inconsistent for a writer who lands on `/success` then clicks through to `/verify`. Same metric, same formatting now. Drift-prevention sibling of the prior `getScoreLabel` / `getSiteUrl` / `buildEmbedSnippets` / `countWords` consolidations.

### 6.13 Streak-correctness + caching + drift-prevention polish (prior revision)
- **DST-safe streak day arithmetic in `lib/history.ts`** — the `summarize()` walker stepped backward through days by subtracting `24 * 60 * 60 * 1000` from the cursor's epoch-ms. Across DST transitions a calendar day is 23h or 25h, so a fixed 24h step can land on the same calendar day (skipping the boundary day) or two days back (counting a missing day as present). The streak counter would have undercounted or overcounted exactly once a year for any writer with an active streak across the spring-forward / fall-back boundaries. Day arithmetic now goes through a `setDate()`-based `addDays()` helper, which always moves by one local calendar day — the same unit `dayKey()` compares. Real correctness fix for the Phase 1.4 surface that just shipped.
- **Caching on `GET /api/documents/[hash]` for found documents** — a certified document is immutable (its hash, content, keystroke trace, and `certified_at` never change after creation), but the verify endpoint was returning every successful response with no `Cache-Control` header. Cold-visitor pageloads from a shared verify link (the embed flywheel target) and external API consumers (Phase 2.1, the just-aligned absolute `verifyUrl`) round-tripped Neon for every view. The route now sets `public, max-age=300, s-maxage=86400, stale-while-revalidate=86400` on found responses so Vercel's edge cache absorbs the load and the database serves only the first-ever fetch per hash + day. 404 responses still have no cache directive, so a revoked or yet-to-be-created hash isn't sticky.
- **Single `countWords()` / `splitWords()` helper in `lib/metrics.ts`** — the same `content.trim().split(/\s+/).filter(Boolean)` regex existed five times: the editor's live word counter (`LockedEditor`), the `Resume draft` banner's word summary (`/write`), the API submission gate (`POST /api/documents`), the `averageWordLength` calculator (`metrics.ts`), and the resume-banner display. Five copies meant the next change to the word-counting rule (whether to count em-dashes as separators, what to do with zero-width joiners, etc.) had five places to drift from — and the 10-word certification gate is exactly the kind of rule we never want to silently disagree about between the client editor and the server submission check. The regex now lives once in `countWords()`/`splitWords()`. Mirrors the prior `getScoreLabel`, `getSiteUrl`, `buildEmbedSnippets` consolidations.

## 8. Stickiness Strategy

The platform is currently a single-shot tool: write once, share once, leave. To increase return-rate and stickiness with the target audience we are investing in:

1. **A persistent author identity** — optional accounts so writers can collect their certified pieces in one shareable profile.
2. **Embeddable verification badges** — a one-line `<script>` writers paste on Substack, Ghost, personal sites — every certified piece on the open web becomes a backlink and a brand impression. *(Phase 1.3, live in all four formats: `/success/<hash>` and `/verify/<hash>` ship copyable **Markdown**, **HTML**, one-line **`<script>`**, and **`iframe`** badges. The script and iframe variants both render a labelled "Verified human-written" pill served by us — from `/embed.js` and `/embed/<hash>` respectively — so the badge in the wild reads as something rather than as an unexplained icon, and its markup stays ours to change after the fact — from **one** definition shared by both served variants, since that promise is only true while there is a single badge to change. The frame variant covers the hosts that strip scripts but allow embeds. Both served variants are now also **cached alike** — the frame was rendered on demand while the script was edge-cached for a day, which billed us per reader impression on the format most likely to succeed — and both are now genuinely **small**. Caching fixed how often we paid for the frame; it did not fix what a *reader* paid. As a Next.js page the frame inherited the root layout and shipped the React runtime, the site stylesheet, two preloaded web fonts, and the full document metadata — ~685 KB to draw a 197×36 pill — and both variants fetched the 49 KB app icon to paint a 22px mark. The frame is now a route handler emitting its own 751-byte document, and the pill draws a 2.7 KB mark: ~3.4 KB for a first-time reader. A badge is a guest on someone else's page and should weigh what a badge weighs. What is missing now is not a format but a **measurement**: we have no count of badges pasted or clicked, so the "embed adoption" metric in §9 has no instrument behind it — and now that the frame is cached, a count has to be taken somewhere the cache doesn't absorb rather than in the page render. The dedicated pill mark is the natural place: it is requested by both served variants and by nothing else on the site, so a request for it already *is* a badge impression.)*
3. **Writing streaks & habits** — gamified "7-day human-written streak" emails turn the certifier into a daily writing ritual. *(Phase 1.4, partially live: `/success/<hash>` shows total certifications, the consecutive-day streak, and a "Best yet" note when the writer ties their longest-ever run; `/write` shows the streak as the writer sits down; `/` now shows it too, above the recall list — so the loop closes on all three surfaces a writer passes through: arrive, sit down, finish. Both `/success` and the landing page also recall the writer's own certified pieces as links to their proofs, with the landing-page list expanding to reach every proof on the device rather than the newest few. All local-first from `localStorage`.)*
4. **Saved drafts** — `localStorage`-backed in-progress drafts so a closed tab doesn't lose work. *(Shipped. The draft survives a failed certification and a backgrounded or discarded mobile tab — the `beforeunload`-only save never ran on iOS Safari, which is where a tab is most likely to disappear — and it is now announced on the landing page, so a returning writer finds the piece they were in the middle of instead of only the ones they finished. Its 24h window is also now stated on both surfaces that announce a draft, rather than expiring in silence, and a draft with no words no longer gates the editor behind a resume prompt it has nothing to offer. The autosave that backs all of this no longer rewrites an unchanged draft on every 3s tick — durability without the blocking main-thread write on the surface the writer is typing into.)*
5. **Native social distribution** — one-click "Post on X" / "Share on LinkedIn" actions auto-write the proof copy so sharing happens inside the success flow. *(Shipped, on both `/success` and `/verify/<hash>`.)*
6. **API + integrations** — a public API for educators, hiring platforms, and CMS plugins (WordPress, Ghost, Substack) so verification happens where writers already work.
7. **Public proof feed** — an opt-in feed of recently certified pieces; signals network activity, drives discovery.

These are detailed and sequenced in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

## 10. Non-Negotiable Principles

1. **No AI-generated text is acceptable on the platform.** The point of the product collapses if pasted AI text gets a high score.
2. **The editor is a writing surface, not a panopticon.** We capture timing, not webcam, not screen, not biometrics.
3. **Verification must be readable by a human in under 10 seconds.** A glance at `/verify/<hash>` should answer the question "was this human-written?" without explanation.
4. **The brand is calm, literary, premium.** Cream backgrounds, deep blue ink, slow easings. The product is not a fraud-detection tool; it is a craftsmanship signal.

## 11. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Adversarial keystroke replay** — someone scripts a "natural" typing cadence to fake a human session. | Future: model human cadence with richer features (digraph timing, error-correction patterns, mouse activity). Track and flag suspiciously regular distributions. |
| **AI dictation tools** — user dictates AI-generated text into the editor. | Server-side check that final content statistical fingerprint matches keystroke trace; future: voice-input detection. |
| **False positives punish slow writers** — careful, deliberate writers may look "robotic." | The score is descriptive, not judgmental. UI messaging emphasizes the keystroke trace itself as the proof, not the score. |
| **Database optionality drift** — `sessionStorage`-only flow breaks when users open the verify link in a new browser. | The `DATABASE_URL` path is the production canonical. Add a clear hosted-DB requirement before paid launch. A configured database now has its schema ensured on the first write (§6.69) and fails certification closed when the INSERT does not land (§6.76), preserving the writer's draft for retry instead of minting a same-session-only proof. A database that is merely *unreachable* also no longer surfaces as a missing document (§6.73): the read path answers `503` and the proof page says the service is unavailable rather than declaring the writer's certified work invalid. |
| **Single-use product** — users certify once and never return. | Stickiness initiatives in §8 / [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). |
