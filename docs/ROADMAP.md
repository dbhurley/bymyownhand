### A certified document reported as a failure + the `iframe` embed badge + a paste notice assistive tech can actually hear (this revision)

The `setItem` now has its own `try`; a failure routes straight to `/verify/<hash>` (which is where `/success/<hash>` sends a visitor with no handoff payload anyway) and records the certification into `lib/history.ts` first — because `/success` is what normally writes that record, and skipping it would silently break the writer's streak and drop the piece out of the proof-recall lists on the one day their storage was full. Verified in a browser against the production build with `sessionStorage.setItem` stubbed to throw: a real 14-word session certified, no error pill, the URL landing on `/verify/bmoh-…`, the draft cleared, and the piece present in the local history and on the landing page's "Your proofs" list. Framable by construction: the `frame-ancestors 'self'` headers are scoped to `/write` and `/success/*`, the input-capturing surfaces that must never be framed. Verified against the production build: the badge renders at 197×36 inside a 210×40 frame on a dark third-party host page with no cream slab and no clipping, `/embed/garbage` renders nothing, `/write` still refuses framing, and both share surfaces show a fourth **iframe** toggle emitting `<iframe src="…/embed/bmoh-…" title="Verified human-written" width="210" height="40" style="border:0" loading="lazy">`. ### The certificate download was broken for a whole class of titles + the one-line `<script>` embed badge + the database schema is actually created (prior revision)
- ✅ **Downloading the PDF certificate failed outright for any document containing an em dash** — the certificate registered three Inter weights from `fonts.gstatic.com`, and those Google-hosted files are *subset* woff2s: embedding an em dash (U+2014) through them throws `RangeError: Offset is outside the bounds of the DataView` inside fontkit, so `pdf().toBlob()` rejected and the writer got *"Could not generate the certificate. Please try again."* — with no retry that could ever succeed. Reproduced in a browser against the running build with the title "Notes — Draft 2": no blob, the `role="alert"` error rendered, stack ending in `EmbeddedFont.embed`. Em dashes in titles are ordinary for the audience this product is built for; the character matrix confirms ASCII, en dash, ellipsis, curly quotes, and accented Latin all render and the em dash never does. Registering remote fonts also made the one durable, portable artifact we hand a writer depend on a **third-party CDN at download time** — it failed offline, behind a content blocker filtering `fonts.gstatic.com`, or on any network that can't reach Google, and every download made a third-party request from the page that had just told the writer their work was their own. The certificate now renders in PDF's built-in standard fonts (Helvetica, plus the Courier it already used for the hash). Verified after the fix with the same em-dash title: a 6,230-byte PDF, no error, **zero** font-CDN requests, and the emitted PDF declaring `/Helvetica`, `/Helvetica-Bold`, `/Courier` with `/WinAnsiEncoding`. `pnpm build` passes.
- ✅ **The Phase 1.3 one-line `<script>` embed badge ships** — the roadmap's headline embed item, and the first badge that *says what it means*. The Markdown and HTML snippets are an `<img>` of the app icon: a 120px blue square a reader has to already recognise, which is a weak impression on the third-party surfaces the flywheel depends on. `/embed.js` renders a calm pill — the hand mark plus "Verified human-written" — linking to `/verify/<hash>`, and because the markup now comes from us rather than from a copied snippet, a change to its wording or styling reaches every embed already in the wild. Dependency-free and inert: it reads its own `data-hash`, rejects anything that doesn't match the hash shape, inserts exactly one anchor where the tag sits, and sets no cookies, reads no storage, and makes no request beyond the badge image — it runs on other people's pages, so the privacy pillar has to hold there too. The link is derived from the origin the script was served from (staging links back to itself) with the canonical host as fallback. Both share surfaces gain a third **Script** toggle, with the labels and hints consolidated into one `EMBED_FORMATS` table instead of four copies of the same sentence. Verified on a plain third-party test page: exactly one badge, in place, `target="_blank" rel="noopener"`, decorative 22px mark loaded, a malformed-hash tag rendering nothing, 0 console errors.
- ✅ **A configured `DATABASE_URL` now actually gets its schema** — `initializeDatabase()` was exported and documented but **never called**, so against a database without the DDL applied by hand (a fresh Neon project, a preview branch, a local Postgres) every `INSERT` failed with `relation "documents" does not exist`, the route swallowed it as an MVP degradation, and the writer was told their document was certified while nothing was persisted — a proof link that resolved only for as long as their own `sessionStorage` survived and 404'd for everyone they shared it with. `ensureSchema()` now runs once per server lifetime on the write path: one cheap `to_regclass()` check, then `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` DDL that is a no-op wherever the schema already exists. A failure is logged and the `INSERT` still attempted (a role without DDL rights should not cost a real writer their certification), and the memo is dropped on failure so a transient error doesn't disable it for the life of the instance. `pnpm build` passes; lint unchanged from baseline (the one pre-existing `/success` setState-in-effect error remains deferred).

### The paste lockdown was reaching nothing on the non-keyboard routes + the writer's own text pastes back there + a streak that says when it is about to break (prior revision)
- ✅ **External paste was being inserted into the certified document, uncounted and unrecorded** — the most serious defect found in the product to date. The guards §6.63 added for the routes with no Cmd/Ctrl+V keydown to intercept (mobile long-press → Paste, `Shift+Insert`, middle-click, Edit ▸ Paste) were registered on `editor.getDomNode()`, one level below Monaco's own clipboard handlers on the editor *container* — which run first on the way down to the focused input and stop the event there. So none of those guards ever ran. Verified before the fix, delivering the event to `.native-edit-context` (what a real paste targets): `PASTED_AI_TEXT_ABC` **inserted into the document**, blocked-paste counter **0**, certified trace **empty** — a finished AI essay could be pasted in on a phone and certified at 100. Same root cause silenced the other two verbs on their real routes: a native cut removed a whole selection and recorded nothing (the §6.65 fix had never fired on the route it was written for), and a non-keyboard copy never populated the internal clipboard. The guards now register on **`window` in the capture phase** — ahead of anything inside the editor's subtree regardless of where Monaco attaches its own — scoped to events targeting this editor and removed on unmount. Verified in a browser: external paste not inserted, one `paste_blocked`, toolbar "1 paste blocked"; native cut records `{delete, Cut, len:5}` with the buffer really losing them; drop still blocked and recorded; Cmd/Ctrl+V still pastes internally exactly once; a keydown-plus-native-paste of one action collapsed by a 250 ms window rather than pasted twice; type-over-selection and plain-Backspace paths unchanged. The blocked-paste `role="status"` notice now fires on these routes too — it never could before. 0 console errors. `pnpm build` passes.
- ✅ **The writer's own copied text pastes back on those same routes** — with the event finally reaching us, the system clipboard is readable at the moment of the attempt, so this route makes the *accurate* version of the judgement the keyboard route has to guess at: allow only when the pasted content is exactly the text copied inside this editor, block everything else regardless of what the internal clipboard holds. Until now a mobile writer moving their own paragraph was blocked and charged 10 integrity points for ordinary editing — the same false positive the non-keyboard `copy` hook exists to prevent, on the way back in. Both halves are recorded through one shared `applyInternalPaste()`, so pasting over a selection records the removal as a `delete` carrying its `len` alongside the insertion. Verified: a non-keyboard copy of "gamma" then a non-keyboard paste over a 7-character selection records `delete/Replace/len=7` + `paste_internal/len=5` with the blocked counter untouched; a paste of different text with that internal clipboard held is blocked and recorded.
- ✅ **The streak says when it is about to break** — an active streak anchors on today *or* yesterday, so a run that has not been extended today reads identically to one that has: `/` showed a healthy "4-day streak" right up to the midnight that silently ended it. `StreakSummary` gains `certifiedToday`, derived from the day set the streak scan already builds, and the landing-page streak line adds a calm *"Write today to keep it going."* Stated as a fact rather than a countdown or a warning colour — a writing ritual, not a game with a life bar — and shown only where the streak is (from two days on). `/` is the surface for it: it is where the writer is standing when the decision gets made, whereas on `/write` they have already made it. Verified in a browser: the last four days excluding today renders "4-day streak · BEST YET / Write today to keep it going."; the same history including today renders the streak line alone; an empty device renders the landing page unchanged; absent from the server-rendered HTML. `pnpm build` passes; lint unchanged from baseline (the one pre-existing `/success` setState-in-effect error remains deferred).

### Replaced selections recorded in the keystroke trace + the writing streak on the landing page + a labeled integrity score in the recall row (prior revision)
- ✅ **Typing, backspacing, or pasting over a selection no longer under-records what it removed** — the prior two revisions closed cut and undo/redo; the replaced selection was the last known gap and the most ordinary of the three. Typing one character over selected text removes the whole selection, but only a `key` event was recorded — so a writer who selected a 400-character paragraph and retyped it left a trace claiming **one character had changed**. A Backspace over a selection recorded a `len`-less `delete`, which every consumer reads as exactly one character. And the *allowed* intra-editor paste over a selection recorded what it inserted and nothing about what it replaced. Same three consequences as the cut and undo/redo holes: the trace stops being the canonical account of the document, `deletionRate` under-reports real revision work (and the integrity score reads it), and Phase 3.4's content-reconstruction check cannot be enforced while any buffer mutation is missing. The selection's size is captured on **keydown** — by keyup Monaco has already applied the edit and the selection is gone — consumed once by the keyup that follows and reset on read, so a keydown whose keyup is skipped (a navigation key, a command combo) can never leak into a later keystroke. **No new event type:** a removal is the `delete` it already is, carrying `len`, with `key: 'Replace'` naming the verb the way `Cut`/`Undo`/`Redo` do, so the trace schema, `isValidKeystrokeEvent()`, and the `/verify` playback are untouched and a plain single-character Backspace still records no `len` (legacy traces replay byte-identically). **Tab is deliberately excluded** — Monaco indents a selection rather than replacing it, and fabricating a deletion that never happened is worse than under-recording a rare keystroke. Verified in a browser, driving Monaco in the real keydown → edit → keyup order: type-over-11 → `delete/Replace/len=11` + `key/KeyQ`; Backspace-over-10 → `delete/Backspace/len=10`; plain Backspace → `len`-less `delete`; plain typing → `key` only, no fabricated delete; internal paste over 17 → `delete/Replace/len=17` + `paste_internal/len=6`; Tab over a selection → `key/Tab` only; and the earlier paths still record exactly once each (blocked paste, cut, undo/redo). 0 console errors. `pnpm build` passes.
- ✅ **The writing streak greets a returning writer on `/`** — the Phase 1.4 habit loop reached `/write` (as the writer sits down) and `/success/<hash>` (right after certifying), but not `/` — the page a returning writer actually lands on, and the one prior revisions turned into the place their body of work lives. The surface that recalled the *work* was the only one that never reflected the *habit*. The "Your proofs" header now carries a calm *"4-day streak"*, with the same **Best yet** recognition `/success` shows when the active run ties the personal best. Costs nothing: `getStreakSummary()` was already called here for the total and its `streak`/`best` discarded. Shown from two days on, matching the other two pills — a "1-day streak" is just today. Verified in a browser: a seeded 4-consecutive-day history renders "4-day streak · BEST YET"; a today-only history renders the heading alone; an empty device renders the landing page unchanged, section and divider absent; and it appears in neither the server-rendered HTML nor, therefore, in front of crawlers.
- ✅ **The recalled proof's integrity score is no longer an unlabeled number** — each row is a single link, so its accessible name is everything inside it, and it ended in a bare figure ("Notes on Proof, Jul 30, 2026, 880 words, 55"): the one number on the row, with no indication of what it measured or meant, on both surfaces the shared row renders. Both the measure and the label are supplied via `sr-only` text, leaving the calm numeral unchanged. Always-on accessibility investment; no visual change. `pnpm build` passes; lint unchanged from baseline (the one pre-existing `/success` setState-in-effect error remains deferred).

### Undo/redo recorded in the keystroke trace + the draft's 24h window made visible + no resume gate for a draft with nothing to resume (prior revision)
- ✅ **Undo and redo no longer change the document without leaving a record** — the prior revision closed cut, the largest hole in the trace; undo and redo were the last two. `Cmd/Ctrl+Z` and `Cmd+Shift+Z` reach Monaco's undo stack directly, and the keyup handler's meta/ctrl guard — there to keep command combos out of the trace as phantom keystrokes — skipped them, so no path in the application saw either verb. Verified before the fix: an undo took the content from **71 characters to 65** and a redo returned it to **71**, while the certified trace stayed at **zero events across both**. Same defect as the cut hole, and it matters for the same three reasons: undo/redo is *ordinary* editing (draft a sentence, undo it, rewrite it) that the canonical record simply did not account for; `deletionRate` under-reported that revision work and the integrity score reads it; and Phase 3.4's content-reconstruction check could not be enforced while any buffer mutation was missing — this was the last one. Hooked on the model-change event rather than the keystrokes, because that is where the mutation and its true size live (one undo step can span several ranges, and its size bears no relation to how many keys were pressed), with `isUndoing`/`isRedoing` scoping it to exactly those two verbs so typing, the allowed internal paste, and a cut stay with their own handlers rather than being double-counted. **No new event type:** a removal is the `delete` it already is, a re-insertion the same bulk insert of the writer's own text `paste_internal` describes, both carrying `len` — so the trace schema, the API's `isValidKeystrokeEvent()` check, and the `/verify` playback (which already honors `len` on both) are untouched, and `key` records which verb it was. Verified in a browser: undo 27 → 20 chars records exactly one `{type:'delete', key:'Undo', pos:21, len:7}`; redo 20 → 27 records exactly one `{type:'paste_internal', key:'Redo', pos:21, len:7}`; a normal edit records nothing through this path; 0 console errors. `pnpm build` passes.
- ✅ **The draft's 24h window is stated instead of enforced in silence** — `DRAFT_MAX_AGE_MS` is a hard deadline that nothing ever mentioned: `loadDraft()` prunes an expired draft on the next read and returns nothing, so a writer who left a substantial piece behind and came back a day later found no draft, no notice, and no explanation — the exact loss Phase 1.1 exists to prevent, arriving through the front door instead of a tab close. Both surfaces that announce a draft (the landing-page recall card, the `/write` resume banner) reported how *old* it was and nothing about how long it had left, which is the half that actually gets a writer back into the editor. One shared `formatDraftExpiry(savedAt)` now renders the remaining window on both, floored at a minute so the last 60 seconds never read "expires in 0m" and returning `null` past the deadline (a draft that far gone is already pruned before either surface renders, so there is nothing to label "expired"). Measured from `savedAt`, which the autosave refreshes every tick, so the window is 24h since the writer last worked on the piece and reopening it extends it. Verified in a browser: the landing card reads "13 words · saved just now · expires in 23h"; the resume banner "(13 words, just now, expires in 23h)".
- ✅ **A draft with nothing to resume no longer gates the editor** — the resume banner is a full-screen interstitial asking one question, "do you want your earlier work back?", which only means something when there *is* earlier work. But autosave persists a snapshot as soon as the writer has a title *or* content, so someone who typed a title and left came back to a card offering to resume a draft of **zero words**, and had to answer it before writing anything: friction on the one surface the product exists to get people onto, and inconsistent with the landing-page card, which already declines to announce a wordless draft as something to return to. A wordless draft is now resumed silently — the writer lands straight in the editor with their title intact and the keystroke trace carried over exactly as pressing *Resume* would have done, which matters because a wordless draft can still hold a real trace (a paragraph typed and deleted), so discarding instead of resuming would have thrown away evidence. Verified in a browser with a seeded title-only draft: the editor renders directly, no interstitial, title already in place. `pnpm build` passes; lint unchanged from baseline (the one pre-existing `/success` setState-in-effect error remains deferred).

### Cut recorded in the keystroke trace + unfinished-draft recall on the landing page + one shared proof-list card (prior revision)
- ✅ **A cut no longer removes text without leaving a record** — the editor handled `Cmd/Ctrl+X` by stashing the selection in the internal clipboard and returning, and the keyup handler's meta/ctrl guard (there to keep command combos out of the trace as phantom keystrokes) then skipped the keyup, so **no path recorded the deletion**. Verified before the fix: cutting a 16-character selection took the content from 63 chars to 47 while the certified trace gained **zero** events — a hole directly under the central claim, since the trace is meant to be the canonical record of how the document came to be and it silently stopped accounting for text the writer removed. `deletionRate` also under-reports real editing (and the integrity score reads it), and Phase 3.4's content-reconstructs-from-trace check had a gap it could never reconcile. Both timestamps now coerce through `Number()`, fall back to `Date.now()` on a non-finite `endedAt`, and persist `0` whenever the resulting window is non-finite or non-positive.

## Phase 3 — Hardening the Proof

### 3.1 Tamper-evident hashing
- Sign each certified document with a server-held key.
- Surface a public verification endpoint: given the document content + keystroke trace, recompute the signature and confirm it matches.

### 3.2 Adversarial-replay defense
- ☐ **Known gap — the Cmd/Ctrl+V route trusts a stale internal clipboard.** The keydown path has to decide whether a paste is the writer's own text *without* being able to read the system clipboard (the keydown is prevented before any clipboard event exists, and an async `navigator.clipboard.readText()` would put a permission prompt in the middle of the writing surface). So it falls back to "did the writer copy something inside this editor?" — which means that after any intra-editor copy, a Cmd+V of *external* content inserts the stale internal text instead of counting a blocked paste. No external text ever enters the document, but the blocked-paste penalty stops firing for someone repeatedly trying, and the writer gets an unexpected old snippet. The non-keyboard routes no longer have this problem (§6.68 compares against the real clipboard content); closing it here means either unifying every route onto the native `paste` event — which trades this for the risk that a route exists where no paste event fires and Monaco inserts unguarded, so it needs verification with a real, trusted paste rather than a synthetic one — or treating a blocked-paste *rate* as its own signal.
- Track digraph timings (the rhythm between specific letter pairs) — much harder to script convincingly than average WPM.
- Flag suspiciously regular distributions (low entropy across multiple sessions from the same account).
- Optional opt-in mouse-movement and focus-event capture for stronger signals (clearly disclosed; off by default).

### 3.3 Voice-input / dictation detection
- Detect bursts of perfectly-spelled multi-word inserts inconsistent with character-by-character keystrokes.
- Allow voice-input as an explicit *mode* (with clear labeling on the certificate) rather than silently allowing it to inflate scores.

### 3.4 Optional content-fingerprint check
- Server-side comparison: does the final content reconstruct from the keystroke trace? Mismatches indicate something post-edited the buffer outside our capture path.
- *Prerequisite, now met:* this check is only meaningful once every mutation of the buffer is in the trace. Three gaps were closed in sequence — **cut** (it removed text and recorded nothing, now a `delete` carrying its `len`), **undo/redo** (hooked on the editor's model-change event so a step's true size is recorded rather than inferred from keystrokes), and **the replaced selection** (typing, backspacing, or internally pasting over selected text removed the whole selection while the trace recorded at most one character; the size is now captured on keydown and recorded as a `delete` carrying its `len`). Every known buffer mutation is therefore accounted for, and this check can be built as an enforced gate rather than an advisory one. Next step is the reconstruction itself: replay the trace server-side and compare the result to the submitted content, then decide the tolerance (an exact match is the goal; IME composition, a Tab that indents rather than replaces a selection, and any future editor affordance are the cases likely to need one).

## What we are deliberately *not* doing

- Real-time collaboration / multiplayer documents (we're a single-author craftsmanship tool).
- AI content scoring of arbitrary uploaded text (we score the *process*, not the output — uploaded text has no process to score).
- Mobile-native apps before the web app's writing experience is best-in-class.
- Identity verification beyond the act of writing itself — no KYC, no biometrics, no webcam.
