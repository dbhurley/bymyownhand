# FTC's New AI Guidelines: A Call for Authenticity in Documents

## The FTC's New Guidelines and Their Implications

This week, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) announced new guidelines for digital platforms centered on user data transparency, particularly concerning how companies disclose AI usage in content generation. While many discussions focus on compliance with these transparency mandates, there is a crucial intersection that businesses must not overlook: the need to demonstrate human authorship in AI-generated documents. This is not merely about ticking boxes; it is about maintaining trust and credibility in a landscape increasingly skeptical of AI outputs.

## Why This Matters

The FTC's guidelines come at a time when the reliance on AI for content creation is at an all-time high. Here’s why the intersection of these guidelines and authentic authorship is significant:

- **Trust Issues**: Consumers are becoming wary of digital content, especially when it is generated by AI. Without clear proof of human authorship, organizations risk losing credibility.
- **Regulatory Compliance**: As we discussed in our post on [Are You Prepared for the EU's Stricter AI Content Regulations?](/blog/eu-stricter-ai-content-regulations), regulatory bodies are tightening scrutiny on how organizations utilize AI. Failing to prove that content is human-generated could result in legal penalties.
- **Operational Risks**: If businesses rely solely on AI for content generation without human oversight, they expose themselves to the risk of producing documents that misrepresent essential information or fail to align with organizational goals.

## The Authenticity Gap

Many organizations are currently focused on meeting compliance requirements but overlook the fundamental aspect of authenticity in their documentation. Here are common pitfalls:

- **Assuming Compliance Equals Credibility**: Just because a document meets regulatory standards does not mean it is viewed as credible by stakeholders. The lack of human insight can lead to a significant credibility gap.
- **Neglecting Verification Mechanisms**: Organizations often fail to establish robust mechanisms to verify human authorship. This is a critical oversight in an era where AI-generated misinformation is rampant.
- **Overemphasis on AI Efficiency**: While leveraging AI tools can enhance productivity, they should not replace human judgment and creativity entirely. The balance between AI efficiency and human input is vital for maintaining trust.

## What You Should Do Differently

To adapt to the FTC’s new guidelines while ensuring the integrity of your documents, consider implementing the following strategies:

1. **Adopt Authenticity Verification Tools**: Utilize platforms such as ByMyOwnHand, which captures keystroke patterns and generates verifiable certificates of authorship. This ensures that your content is not only compliant but also genuinely authored by humans.
2. **Train Teams on AI and Compliance**: Ensure that your teams understand the implications of the FTC guidelines and how to integrate authenticity verification into their workflows. This includes educating them on the importance of maintaining a human touch in AI-generated content.
3. **Establish Clear Documentation Processes**: Create a workflow that emphasizes the necessity of verifying human authorship in critical documents. This should be a standard part of your compliance strategy moving forward.

## Conclusion

The FTC's new guidelines are a wake-up call for organizations that rely on AI for content generation. By recognizing the critical need for authenticity alongside compliance, businesses can better navigate this evolving landscape. Trust and credibility are paramount; therefore, ensuring that your documents are genuinely authored by humans is not just a regulatory requirement—it is essential for maintaining stakeholder confidence.
