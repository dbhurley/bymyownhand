# The EU's DSA: Missing the Mark on Human Content Verification

## The DSA Announcement: What You Need to Know

This week, the European Union made headlines with the announcement of the Digital Services Act (DSA), a sweeping piece of legislation aimed at enhancing accountability for content moderation across digital platforms. While the DSA is a significant step toward responsible online behavior, it has one glaring oversight: it does not adequately emphasize the need for verification of human authorship in content creation. This gap presents a unique opportunity for businesses to adopt solutions that ensure human authorship verification, thereby enhancing compliance and trust with users.

## Why the DSA Matters

The DSA seeks to hold platforms accountable for the content they host, addressing issues like misinformation, illegal content, and user safety. Here are some key aspects of the DSA:
- **Accountability for Content Moderation:** Platforms must take responsibility for the content they curate, ensuring it complies with EU standards.
- **Transparency in Algorithms:** Platforms must disclose how their algorithms function, particularly regarding content recommendation and moderation.
- **User Protection:** The DSA aims to protect users from harmful content and misinformation, creating a safer online environment.

However, amid these commendable goals, there is a critical aspect that has been overlooked: the verification of human authorship in the content being shared.

## The Missing Piece: Human Authorship Verification

As organizations navigate the complexities of the DSA, they must also confront the reality that much of the content generated today involves AI. While algorithms can create content at scale, relying solely on these systems without clear verification of human involvement raises significant concerns:
- **Trust Erosion:** Consumers are increasingly skeptical about the reliability of AI-generated content. If organizations cannot prove that their documents are human-authored, they risk losing credibility. A recent Pew Research survey indicated that 69% of Americans are concerned about the reliability of AI-generated content.
- **Compliance Risks:** The DSA's requirements will likely lead to stricter regulations around accountability. If your organization cannot demonstrate that your content aligns with human authorship, you expose yourself to legal repercussions.
- **Operational Integrity:** Without mechanisms to verify human involvement in content creation, organizations risk producing misleading or harmful information.

## Opportunities for Businesses

The gap in the DSA regarding human authorship verification allows businesses to differentiate themselves by implementing robust verification solutions. Here are actionable steps organizations can take:
1. **Adopt Verification Tools:** Implement tools that verify authorship and ensure that content is human-generated. Solutions like ByMyOwnHand can assist in capturing keystroke data and ensuring authenticity.
2. **Educate Your Team:** Ensure your team understands the importance of human authorship verification and its implications for compliance with the DSA.
3. **Engage in Dialogue:** Participate in discussions about the DSA and advocate for the inclusion of human authorship verification in future regulations. Engaging with industry peers and regulators can help shape the conversation.
4. **Build Trust with Your Audience:** Communicate transparently with users about your content verification processes. This transparency can enhance consumer confidence in your brand.

## Conclusion: A Path Forward

As the EU moves forward with the DSA, organizations must seize this moment to establish frameworks for human authorship verification. By addressing this gap, businesses can protect their reputation, comply with evolving regulations, and build lasting trust with users.
